# SkySift Dossiers: design

Date: 20 October 2026. Status: draft for review. Builds on `2026-10-20-skysift-mission-control-design.md` (the dashboard) and its recheck plan (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-20-skysift-recheck.md`), which must land first: a dossier is generated from the recheck's outputs.

## 1. Intent

Today the one candidate dossier (KIC 6146676) is a hand-built page under `docs/candidates/`, assembled by scripts that call the engine's own functions, and nothing in the dashboard points at it. The dashboard should own this step: a **Dossiers** page shows the final set of candidates as tiles, each with two separate, labelled probabilities, and a click opens that candidate's dossier at Normal or Expert level. Every dossier is generated by one engine command from stored results, so the page for the next candidate costs nothing by hand and every number on it can be traced to a stored row or a logged query.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals
- One command, `skysift dossier <target>`, produces a dossier from the database and the cached files, in one to three minutes, offline except for the catalogue queries the dossier itself cites.
- The Dossiers page lists the final set: every `candidate_stage2` signal and every reviewer-approved signal under the current search version. A stage-1 candidate can be given a dossier on demand.
- Two probabilities per candidate, computed by the engine, with their assumptions printed on the dossier: the statistical false-alarm probability and an approximate astrophysical false-positive probability.
- The dossier keeps the structure, voice and two levels of the KIC 6146676 page (sections 01 to 08), including the illustrated "How it might look" panel.
- A dossier can be exported as one standalone HTML file for sharing.

Non-goals
- Accounts, public hosting, PDF export, TESS, radial velocities or imaging follow-up.
- A full vespa-style FPP with PRF fits and galactic models (section 4.2 is an explicit approximation, labelled as such).

Anti-goals
- A single blended "score" with no definition. Each number has a name, a formula and a sentence saying what it does not cover.
- "Discovery" or "confirmed" wording anywhere before a human approval. The dossier of an unapproved candidate says "candidate" throughout.

## 3. Who it is for

The same two readers as the dashboard: Nova (phone, Normal level, wants the story and the picture) and the vetting astronomer (Expert level, wants every number, its error and where it came from). The export is for a third reader: someone outside the laptop who receives the HTML file.

## 4. The two probabilities

### 4.1 False-alarm probability (FAP), statistical

What it answers: how often does noise alone, on a light curve like this one, produce a top peak at least this strong?

Source: the null calibration (`skysift calibrate`, kinds `inverted` and `scrambled`) for the same source and search version. Let `n_null` be the number of null searches and `k` the number whose top SDE is at or above the candidate's SDE.

- `FAP = (k + 1) / (n_null + 1)` (the add-one rule, so a candidate above every null reads as "less than 1 in n_null + 1", never zero).
- Shown as "1 in N" with N rounded to two significant figures, plus the raw counts in Expert ("0 of 1,716 null searches reached SDE 50.5").
- Validity: computed only when `n_null >= 200` for that source and version; otherwise the tile shows "not yet calibrated" and the dossier explains that the pilot is still running. A mismatch between the candidate's search version and the calibration's version shows "calibration is for an older search" and still prints the number in Expert.
- What it does not cover, printed under the number: "This is about noise. A real dip on a neighbouring star or an eclipsing binary passes this test."

Where the counts come from: `calibrate.read_rows` on the calibration output file for the source (`calibrate.default_path`), summarised by a new `calibrate.false_alarm(rows, sde, version) -> FalseAlarm(k, n, fap, version_matches)`.

### 4.2 Astrophysical false-positive probability (FPP), approximate

What it answers: given that the dip is real, how likely is it to be something other than a planet on this star?

Model: a scenario count in the spirit of Morton (2012), reduced to three scenarios. Each scenario gets a weight `w = prior x geometric probability x data factor`; `FPP = (w_BEB + w_EB) / (w_PL + w_BEB + w_EB)`. The constants live in `skysift/priors.py` with their citations, and every factor is printed in a table on the Expert level.

| Scenario | Prior (per star) | Geometric probability | Data factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planet on the target (PL) | `f_pl(Rp, P)`: the occurrence rate per star in the radius and period bin, from the Dressing and Charbonneau 2015 grid for Teff below 4,000 K and the Fressin et al. 2013 grid otherwise, linearly interpolated in log P and log Rp and held at the nearest edge outside the grids | `R_star / a` for the circular orbit at this period and stellar density | `D_shape`: the rung 2 fit's duration against the circular duration at the star's density, as `exp(-z^2 / 2)` for `z = (T14_fit - T14_circ) / sigma`, with `sigma` the quadrature of the fit error and 30 percent (eccentricity allowance); 1 when the fit is absent |
| Background or foreground eclipsing binary within the confusion radius (BEB) | `sum over neighbours` of `f_EB x e_i`: `f_EB = 0.014` (the Kepler EB catalogue fraction, Kirk et al. 2016), and `e_i = 1` when neighbour `i` is bright enough to produce the depth (its flux fraction in the aperture, `10^(-0.4 (G_i - G_target))` diluted by the target, must exceed twice the depth for a 50 percent eclipse) else 0 | `(R1 + R2) / a` for an equal-mass pair at twice the period (an EB's true period is 2P when the primary and secondary look alike) | `X_i`: the pixel exclusion of neighbour `i`: `exp(-k_i^2 / 2)` with `k_i = max(0, (s_i - r_c) / sigma_c)` where `s_i` is its separation from the season-averaged difference centroid, `sigma_c` the centroid error and `r_c` the confusion radius; inside `r_c` the factor is 1 |
| Eclipsing binary on the target itself, hierarchical or grazing (EB) | `f_bin x g(P)`: the binary fraction of the spectral type (0.26 for M dwarfs, Winters et al. 2019; 0.44 for FGK, Raghavan et al. 2010) times the fraction of binaries with period within a factor of two of 2P from the log-normal period distribution of Raghavan et al. 2010 | `(R1 + R2) / a` at 2P with `R2 = 0.5 R1` | `S_sec x S_oe x S_v`: the secondary limit (an equal-period EB of this depth predicts a secondary of at least `depth x 0.1`; the factor is the Gaussian tail of that prediction against the measured secondary and its error, from the rung 2 metrics `secondary_ppm` and the control scatter), the odd/even limit (the predicted alternation for `R2 = 0.5 R1` is 25 percent of the depth; the factor is its Gaussian tail against `odd_ppm`, `even_ppm` and their errors) and the V-shape limit (`fit_b` below 0.9 gives 1; above it the factor is 0.5, since a grazing geometry is then possible for both) |

Rules:
- The confusion radius `r_c` is the larger of three times the season-averaged centroid error and half a Kepler pixel (2.0 arcsec): the Gaussian PSF fit cannot separate sources closer than that. Without a pixel stage, `r_c` is one Kepler pixel (3.98 arcsec), `X_i = 1` for every neighbour inside it and `exp(-(s_i - r_c)^2 / (2 x 1.3^2))` outside it (the KBonus PSF photometry itself limits blends at the 1.3 arcsec level only loosely), and the tile labels the number "FPP (no pixel data yet)".
- Neighbours come from one Gaia DR3 cone query of 20 arcsec (five pixels) around the target, logged like every other query, keeping sources with `G <= G_target + 2.5 log10(1 / (2 x depth))` (fainter stars cannot make the dip even when fully eclipsed).
- Expected scale, to be confirmed on the two known stars before the numbers go on a tile: for KIC 6146676 (every neighbour excluded at 4.6 sigma or more, rung 2 clean) the BEB term should fall to the order of 10^-4 and the EB term to the order of 10^-3 against a PL weight near 10^-3, giving an FPP of a few tens of percent at most, dominated by the on-target EB prior at this period. If the first numbers fall outside 1 to 60 percent for both stars, the priors are re-examined before the page shows them; the review gate is Vikram's sign-off on the factor tables of these two stars, after which they become the golden tests.
- Shown as a percentage with one significant figure ("about 3 percent"), never below "under 1 percent" and never above "over 90 percent".
- What it does not cover, printed under the number: "This counts the common impostors with simple priors. It is not a vespa or a Kepler DR25 false-positive probability, and it has no radial-velocity or imaging input."
- The computation lives in `skysift/fpp.py`, pure, with a dataclass result and a test on the KIC 6146676 and KIC 5689121 numbers (the dossier values become the golden values once reviewed by Vikram).

### 4.3 How the tile reads them

Two separate pills with names, never a combined score:

> **False alarm** 1 in 1,700  |  **False positive** about 3 percent

Each pill opens the same verdict dialog as the dashboard with a "Probabilities" section explaining both in two sentences and linking to the dossier's section 03.

## 5. The dossier

### 5.1 Content, section by section (from the KIC 6146676 template)

| Section | Normal level | Expert adds | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 The signal | period, depth, duration, SDE tiles; one-sentence summary; the folded transit | the periodogram with the alias lines; the prewhitening record | signal row, `plots.build_plots`, the stored periodogram summary |
| 02 Does it repeat | the event gallery (every transit with data, ±8 h), the odd/even fold, seasons | per-event depths with errors, the event-quality numbers | `lc.ops.event_depths`, `event_quality`, the rung 1 events and rung 3 metrics |
| 03 The checks | the ladder as the dashboard's Why list, plus the two probabilities with their sentences | the raw metrics, the FAP counts, the FPP factor table | `explain.why`, `calibrate.false_alarm`, `fpp.compute` |
| 04 Which star is dimming | the pixel verdict in words, the season centroid figure, the leak test sentence | per-season difference images as S/N maps, the two-source fit numbers, the TPF manifest | rung 5 metrics and its stored `pixels.npz` artifact (section 5.8) |
| 05 The star and the planet | spectral type, distance, Teff, radius, mass; planet radius, orbit, equilibrium temperature, insolation, with errors | the Gaia and catalogue rows used, the reddening caveat with the alternative radius | `stars.py` (section 5.3) |
| 06 How it might look | the illustrated panel (star colour from Teff, planet size and colour from radius and Teq, the habitable-zone bar) | the formulas behind the panel | computed in the template's script from section 05 numbers, as today |
| 07 What is not yet known | the caveats list generated from the gaps: no pixel stage, no spectrum, reddening, single season, and so on | the same with the numbers | rules in `dossier.py` |
| 08 How to reproduce | the commands, the search and ladder versions, the query log count, the file hashes | the full query log for this target | `queries` table, run rows, artifact rows |

The archive section of the old context work (KOI, TCE, TOI, EB catalogues, Planet Hunters, SIMBAD) is rung 4's job: section 03 shows rung 4's catalogue results; the dossier does not re-query them.

### 5.2 Levels and the dashboard

The dossier is a dashboard page, `/dossiers/<target_id>`, rendered from the stored `dossier_data.json` with the dashboard's base shell, tokens and mode cookie: Normal is the dashboard's simple mode, Expert its expert mode. The old page's own toggle is gone; the mode control in the top bar does it. The standalone export (`skysift dossier <target> --export <file>`) inlines the stylesheet, the fonts as data URIs, the JSON and the scripts into one file with its own toggle, as the KIC 6146676 page has today.

### 5.3 Stellar and planet parameters (`skysift/stars.py`)

New pure-plus-fetch module. Inputs: the target row and its light-curve meta (KIC id, Gaia DR3 id, G magnitude). Queries, each logged through `net.fetch` and cached by the blob, all through the Gaia archive TAP (one service, already in rung 4's allow list): `gaiadr3.gaia_source` (parallax and its error, `G`, `BP`, `RP`, `ruwe`, `teff_gspphot`, `radius_flame`, `mass_flame`), `external.gaiaedr3_distance` (Bailer-Jones et al. 2021 geometric distance), and the 2MASS cross-match (`gaiadr3.tmass_psc_xsc_best_neighbour` joined to `gaiadr1.tmass_original_valid`) for `Ks`. No ExoFOP, SIMBAD or VizieR calls.

The primary route is the one the KIC 6146676 dossier used (`docs/candidates/kic-006146676/context/d_stellar_planet.py`), so the generator reproduces its numbers: parallax with the Lindegren et al. 2021 zero-point correction (the `gaiadr3-zeropoint` package is already in the environment; vendor the few lines rather than importing it in the engine), the Mamajek dwarf-sequence table at the observed BP minus RP with the reddening assumption E(BP minus RP) at or below 0.1 for Teff and spectral type, and for Teff below 4,000 K the Mann et al. 2015 radius and Mann et al. 2019 mass relations from the absolute Ks; above 4,000 K the Mamajek radius and mass at that colour. Errors by a 20,000-draw Monte Carlo over parallax, photometry and the relations' scatter, reported as the 16th to 84th percentile. Gaia FLAME and GSP-Phot values, when present, are shown in Expert as the cross-check with a sentence on the difference, never as the headline. Fallback when Ks is missing: the Mamajek radius and mass at the colour, with the relation scatter doubled and a caveat line in section 07.

Derived: stellar density, planet radius from the fitted `k` (rung 2) with the stellar radius error propagated through the same draws, semi-major axis from the period and mass, equilibrium temperature at albedo 0.3 with full redistribution, insolation in Earth units, the Kopparapu et al. 2013 habitable-zone edges. The reddening caveat: when E(BP minus RP) is unknown, print the alternative radius at E = 0.2 as the dossier does now. Every value carries its error and its source string.

### 5.4 Generation (`skysift/dossier.py`, `skysift dossier`)

`skysift dossier --target <id> [--signal <id>] [--export <file>] [--force]`:

1. Pick the signal: the given id, else the target's best signal by status order (`candidate_stage2`, approved, `candidate_stage1`) and SDE.
2. Load the light curve through `lc.loader`, recompute the whitened flux as the sweep did, and produce the plot arrays (thinned to the same bin sizes as the old `prep_data.py`: 0.25 d bins for the full curve, ±8 h event gallery, 1,500 log bins for the spectrum).
3. Collect the test rows (latest per rung), the recheck history, rung 5's `pixels.npz` artifact when present.
4. `stars.lookup` (section 5.3), `calibrate.false_alarm`, `fpp.compute`.
5. Write `<data_dir>/dossiers/<target_id>/dossier_data.json` (all floats rounded to what the page prints; arrays as lists) and insert or update a `dossiers` row: `target_id`, `signal_id`, `generated` (UTC), `data_path`, `search_version`, `ladder_version`, `fap`, `fpp`, `tests_fingerprint` (hash of the test row ids used), `export_path`.
6. With `--export`, render the standalone file.

The Dossiers page's "Generate" button runs the same command as a background job through the existing launcher pattern (`ui/sweeps.py` style: a lock, a log, BelowNormal priority), one at a time; the tile shows "generating" and polls.

Staleness: a dossier is stale when the signal has newer test rows than its fingerprint, or the signal's status changed. The tile shows "stale, regenerate", the dossier page a banner. The recheck marks dossiers stale when it writes rows for their signals.

### 5.5 Pages

**Dossiers** (`/dossiers`, in `NAV` between Candidates and Live): a grid of tiles, one per candidate in the final set plus any stage-1 candidate that already has a dossier. Tile: the illustration thumbnail (the planet from section 06, drawn as a 96 px SVG from the stored parameters), target name and the spectral type and distance line, the one-sentence summary, the two probability pills, the status pill, the generated date or the Generate button, the stale note. Sort: approved first, then stage 2, then stage 1; within a group by FPP ascending. Empty state: "No dossiers yet. A dossier is generated for a stage-2 candidate or an approved one. Run a sweep, then a recheck." with links.

**Dossier** (`/dossiers/<target_id>`): the eight sections at the dashboard's mode, with a sticky section index on wide screens and the bottom nav on phones, a "Download standalone page" button (serves the export, generating it on demand), the decision bar for a reviewer (same route as the signal page), and "Open in the candidate queue" back to the signal page.

**Approved** page: each entry gains a "Dossier" link when one exists.

### 5.6 Data and files

- Table `dossiers` (migration step `003_dossiers`): columns as in 5.4 plus `fpp_detail` (JSON, the factor table) and `fap_detail` (JSON, counts and versions).
- Artifacts: `dossier_data.json` and the export are recorded as artifacts of kind `dossier`, pinned.
- Fonts in the export come from the dashboard's vendored woff2 files as data URIs (about 130 KB).

### 5.9 The submission pack (`skysift pack`)

There is no NASA portal that accepts a new Kepler candidate from outside a mission: the NASA Exoplanet Archive ingests planets from the refereed literature and adds candidates only when a mission delivers them; ExoFOP (NExScI) is the community follow-up site, where registered users upload files, notes and parameters, and Kepler and K2 follow-up data there now sits in ExoFOP-TESS under TIC identifiers; a community TOI needs TESS evidence. The routes a candidate can take are therefore: a Research Note of the AAS or a journal paper (the archive picks it up); an ExoFOP-TESS upload of the dossier, the ephemeris and a note under the star's TIC id; a Zenodo deposit for a citable DOI and a frozen record; and a help-desk note to the archive once published.

`skysift pack --target <id> [--out <dir>] [--zenodo]` builds one folder (and a zip) with everything those routes need, from the dossier's stored data:

- `README.md`: the step-by-step instructions, in order: 1 freeze the record (Zenodo), 2 upload to ExoFOP-TESS (TIC id from the Gaia or KIC cross-match, which files to attach, what the note should say, the password-protection choice), 3 write up (the Research Note draft below, the AAS submission steps, arXiv endorsement), 4 tell the archive (the help-desk address and the one-paragraph template), 5 what to answer when asked for follow-up. Each step names the account the person needs and says plainly that the engine cannot do it for them, except step 1.
- `note/`: an AASTeX Research Note draft (under 1,000 words, one figure) filled from the dossier: title, abstract, the detection and vetting paragraph with the ladder numbers, the pixel localisation paragraph, the star and planet table, the caveats, the data-availability statement with the Zenodo DOI placeholder, the citations the dossier used.
- `data/`: the detrended light curve (CSV: time BKJD, flux, error, quarter), the ephemeris and parameters (CSV and JSON with errors), the per-event depths, the periodogram summary, the rung metrics (JSON).
- `figures/`: the folded transit, the odd and even fold, the periodogram, the per-season centroid figure, as PNG and SVG.
- `dossier.html`: the standalone export.
- `provenance/`: search and ladder versions, the git commit, the query log for this target (every catalogue and data request with URL, time, status and SHA-256), the file hashes, the calibration counts behind the FAP.
- `exofop/`: the files renamed to ExoFOP's convention (`<TIC>_<UTC date>_<observer tag>_<content>.<ext>`) and `note.txt` with the upload text.
  Amended 21 October 2026: ExoFOP takes KIC ids directly and enforces no file name, so the TIC-based names were dropped; the copies are named `<target>_<yyyymmdd>_<content>.<ext>` and the uploader sets the Data Tag (`YYYYMMDD_username_description_nnnnn`) on the upload form.

With `--zenodo` and `SKYSIFT_ZENODO_TOKEN` in the environment, the command creates a Zenodo deposition through the API (sandbox by default, `--zenodo-live` for the real site), uploads the pack, fills the metadata (title, creators from `CITATION.cff`, description from the dossier summary, keywords, related identifiers) and stops before publishing: publishing is a click the person makes on Zenodo after reading the record, and the command prints the draft URL. The candidate row then gets `pack_path` and, after the person publishes, `doi` through the dashboard's existing decision flow ("published" with the DOI pasted in). The engine never posts to ExoFOP, arXiv or a journal.

The Dossiers page gets a "Build submission pack" button on approved candidates (the same background-job pattern) and a link to the pack once built; the dossier page shows the pack's step list with a tick for the steps the person has marked done (stored in the `dossiers` row as `pack_steps`).

## 6. Behaviour that does not change

The engine's search version, the ladder, the recheck, the candidate queue, decisions. A dossier never changes a status; a reviewer's decision on the dossier page is the same append-only decision.

## 7. Accessibility and responsiveness

As the dashboard: the tile grid 1/2/3 columns at 360/720/1080 px; every probability pill carries its word; the illustration has an `alt` sentence ("A cool red star with a planet about two and a half times the size of Earth, in the habitable zone"); the sticky section index is a `nav` with the section names; no em-dashes.

## 8. Testing

Unit: `calibrate.false_alarm` on a synthetic rows file (add-one rule, version mismatch, below-200 rule); `fpp.compute` on hand-built inputs with the three scenarios isolated, plus the golden values for KIC 6146676 and KIC 5689121 once reviewed; `stars.derive` on known inputs (the KIC 6146676 numbers from the dossier: Rp 2.61 R⊕, a 0.182 AU, Teq 267 K); `dossier.generate` end to end on the seeded database with a synthetic light curve and a fake fetch (writes the JSON, the row, the fingerprint; a second run is a no-op; `--force` regenerates); the two pages in both modes and themes with and without a dossier; the export is a single file with no external URL. Visual: the dossier page and the tile grid at the four widths.

### 5.7 What the generator owes the hand-built page

The generated dossier uses a plain templated voice: every sentence is built from stored numbers by a rule ("The difference-image centroid sits 0.15 pixels from the candidate and 2.9 pixels from KIC 6146666 in every season with data"). The hand-written narrative of the KIC 6146676 page (its ledes, the notes on particular neighbours, the host-identity caveat) is not reproduced; those paragraphs stay in `docs/candidates/` as the record. The one-time comparison in section 9 is numeric: period, depth, duration, Rp, a, Teq, the per-season centroid offsets and the two-source depths must agree with the hand-built values within their quoted errors, and the differences are written to the generator's report.

### 5.8 Dependency on the recheck plan

The recheck plan is the contract: `Localisation.metrics()` keys, `rung5_pixels` reasons, `Store.latest_tests`, `lc.loader`, ladder version "4". One amendment to it, made now: rung 5 stores the arrays the dossier draws (per-season `diff`, `var`, `direct`, `valid`, the pixel positions of both stars and the season list) as a `pixels.npz` artifact of kind `pixels`, pinned with the signal, so the dossier never recomputes difference images or re-downloads pixel files. Generation stays offline except for the Gaia queries of section 5.3.

## 9. Open calls made here (for Vikram to check)

- The final set is stage 2 plus reviewer-approved; stage 1 is on demand. (Alternative: every stage 1 too, which would put rediscoveries and aliases on the page.)
- FAP uses the add-one rule and needs at least 200 null searches of the same source.
- FPP is the three-scenario approximation of section 4.2 with the priors and factors written there, labelled approximate, with the factor table always visible in Expert, gated by your sign-off on the two known stars' factor tables before the numbers appear on a tile.
- Stellar parameters follow the route of the hand-built dossier (Mamajek colour table, Mann relations for M dwarfs, Gaia parallax with the zero-point correction, Monte Carlo errors), with Gaia's own FLAME values as an Expert cross-check only.
- The generated page uses a templated voice; the hand-written narrative is not reproduced, and the comparison with the hand-built page is numeric.
- Rung 5 gains a `pixels.npz` artifact (an amendment to the recheck plan).
- The dossier is rendered from stored JSON inside the dashboard; the standalone HTML is an export, not the source of truth.
- The old `docs/candidates/kic-006146676/` folder stays as the record of the hand-built page; the generated dossier for that star replaces it on the dashboard.

## 10. Out of scope

Vespa-style FPP, PRF fits, radial velocities, imaging, TESS, PDF, public hosting, automatic approval.
