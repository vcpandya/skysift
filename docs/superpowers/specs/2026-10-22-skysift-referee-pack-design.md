# SkySift referee-ready pack: design

Date: 22 October 2026. Status: decided and recorded (Vikram delegated the scientific calls; human review comes before anything is published). Amends section 5.9 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-skysift-dossiers-design.md` (the pack) and section 4.4 of the mission-control spec (the pixel rung's error). The research behind every row is `docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-22-referee-checklist.md`; this document says what gets built for the rows that are not met.

## 1. Intent

The submission pack of 21 October holds the candidate's story, its two probabilities and its provenance, but it quotes the ephemeris and the transit shape without errors, carries no independent false-positive estimate, and its centroid error has no systematic floor. A referee, an ExoFOP upload form and the archive's parameter template all ask for those three things first. This work adds them, and the smaller items the checklist found (odd and even depths with errors, a secondary-eclipse limit, stellar provenance, dilution numbers, a code-availability statement, truthful route wording), so that the pack of KIC 6146676 could go to a reviewer as it is.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals
- Period, mid-transit epoch, depth, duration, radius ratio, impact parameter and a/R* each carry a 1-sigma error, from a method with a named precedent, stored on the dossier and printed on the page, in the ExoFOP files, the archive email and the Research Note.
- An independent false-positive probability from TRICERATOPS for KIC 6146676 and KIC 5689121, stored beside SkySift's own, with the comparison written down.
- The pixel rung's per-season centroid error carries a measured systematic floor, and the live verdicts are rerun under it.
- The live Zenodo gate refuses a record that lacks the errors or the cross-check.
- Every change is a stored number the page, the pack and the note read; nothing is typed by hand.

Non-goals
- A full MCMC transit fit with limb darkening (the uniform-disc model stays; its limits are stated).
- Replacing SkySift's scenario FPP with TRICERATOPS. The cross-check is a second opinion, printed beside the first.
- The Kepler PRF, a TIC lookup, an ExoFOP upload, or any network action from the pack.

Anti-goals
- An error that is smaller than the scatter it summarises (every error is inflated by the reduced chi-square when that exceeds one).
- A cross-check that silently shares the engine's numerics: TRICERATOPS runs in its own environment with its own numpy, and the engine's venv is untouched.
- A floor that hides a known bias: a registration shift the metadata already knows is reported as such, not folded into a constant.

## 3. Ephemeris and transit-parameter errors (`skysift/ephemeris.py`)

### 3.1 Method and precedent

Two methods were on the table: a bootstrap over transit times for everything, or a per-transit linear fit for the ephemeris with a bootstrap over transits for the shape. The second is chosen.

- The period and the epoch come from a weighted linear least-squares fit of the per-transit mid-times, with the covariance of that fit. This is how the Kepler transit-timing catalogue quotes linear ephemerides (Holczer et al. 2016) and how Deeg 2015 says they should be quoted: the reference epoch at the centre of the series, where its error is smallest, and the prediction error at a future epoch from the period error and the distance from that centre. A bootstrap over transit times gives the same covariance with more noise and no closed form for predictions, so it is not used for the ephemeris.
- The depth, duration, radius ratio, impact parameter and a/R* come from a bootstrap over transits: the events are resampled with replacement and the uniform-disc fit is run on each resample. This follows the Planet Hunters practice (Schmitt et al. 2014: "the error bars are estimated with a bootstrapping method"). The binned least-squares fit's own covariance is not used because the fold's bins hide red noise and the k-b degeneracy, and because a bootstrap over whole transits carries the transit-to-transit scatter the per-event depths already show.

### 3.2 Per-transit times

For each epoch E whose window holds data (at least `EVENT_IN_MIN` = 3 good cadences within half a duration of the predicted mid-time and `EVENT_OUT_MIN` = 6 within one to three durations, at least 2 on each side, the pixel rung's rules), the mid-time is the shift that minimises the chi-square of the whitened flux against a fixed template: the rung 2 uniform-disc model with its fitted k, b and a/R* when `fit_ok` is true, else the search box of the stored depth and duration. The template's depth is fixed (the per-event depths are a separate measurement). The shift runs over a grid of one minute steps across plus and minus one duration; the chi-square minimum is refined by a parabola through the three lowest points, the error is the half-width at delta chi-square of one, the per-point error is 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation of the window's out-of-transit flux, and the error is inflated by the square root of the reduced chi-square when that exceeds one. An event whose chi-square curve has no minimum inside the grid, or whose shift error exceeds half a duration, is dropped with a reason. Times are BKJD; the stored table carries BKJD and BJD_TDB (plus 2,454,833).

Outliers: after a first linear fit, events more than 5 median absolute deviations from the line (the Holczer et al. 2016 rule) are dropped and the fit is made again; dropped events stay in the table with `used` false and the reason.

### 3.3 The linear ephemeris

Weighted least squares of T_E = T_ref + P (E - E_ref), with E_ref the error-weighted mean epoch of the used events rounded to the nearest integer (Deeg 2015's central epoch). Outputs: P and sigma_P, T_ref and sigma_Tref, their covariance, the chi-square and the number of degrees of freedom, the rms of the residuals in minutes, and the number of events used. Errors are inflated by the square root of the reduced chi-square when it exceeds one. T0 at epoch 0 (the first transit, the epoch ExoFOP and the archive want) is T_ref - P E_ref with sigma squared equal to sigma_Tref squared plus E_ref squared sigma_P squared minus 2 E_ref times the covariance. A prediction at epoch E has sigma squared equal to sigma_Tref squared plus (E - E_ref) squared sigma_P squared (Deeg 2015, Eq. 18, in the general form). With fewer than 3 used events there is no ephemeris error, and the dossier says so.

### 3.4 Bootstrap over transits

`N_BOOT` = 200 resamples (a parameter; tests use 20). Each resample draws the used epochs with replacement, concatenates their points, and runs `transit_model.fit_transit` with the signal's period, T0 and duration. From the resamples that converge: the 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of k, b, a/R*, T14 (hours) and the model depth (k squared, in ppm). The point estimate stays the rung 2 fit (the dossier does not re-fit the full curve); the errors were first written as the distances from the rung 2 value to the resample percentiles, floored at zero; ruling of 22 October 2026 (Task 1 re-review): a full-curve fit that sits at the edge of the resample distribution then reads "+0.000" on one side, which is not a 1-sigma error a referee can read, so the errors are the resample spread itself, minus = p50 - p16 and plus = p84 - p50, with the bootstrap median stored beside the point value and a dossier caveat when the point lies further from the bootstrap median than the larger half-width. When the rung 2 fit is absent or failed, the shape block carries only the box depth and duration: the depth is the per-event weighted mean with its scaled error (rung 2's `depth_ppm`, `depth_err_ppm`), the duration is the box width that maximises the depth-to-error ratio on each resample over a grid from half to twice the search duration, with its percentiles. Fewer than 20 converged resamples give no shape errors, with a reason. Ruling of 22 October 2026 (Task 1 review): at the 29.4-minute long cadence the uniform-disc fit is degenerate between k, b and a/R* whenever the ingress is not resolved, and a bootstrap over transits then measures the scatter around one local solution, not the width of the valley (on the module's test scene the injected b lay 8 sigma outside the bootstrap interval). The shape block therefore computes the ingress duration from the fitted geometry, (T14 - T23) / 2 with the uniform-disc contact times, and when it is shorter than the cadence (`CADENCE_MIN` = 29.4) marks the block `degenerate`, keeps the b and a/R* point values, quotes no error for them with the reason printed, and keeps the bootstrap errors of k, T14 and the model depth, which the depth and the box width constrain (Kipping 2010, MNRAS 408, 1758, on binning and the ingress).

### 3.5 Odd and even, secondary

- Odd and even depths with errors: from the per-event box depths and errors (`lc.ops.event_depths`), the weighted mean per parity with its error inflated by the square root of the parity's reduced chi-square; the difference in sigma from the two errors in quadrature. Rung 2's `parity_sigma` stays the vetting number; this block is the reporting number and the dossier prints both when they differ by more than 0.5 sigma.
- Secondary: the depth at phase 0.5 with its error (rung 2's `secondary_ppm` and `control_rms_ppm`), its 3-sigma upper limit (max(measured, 0) plus three errors), the strongest secondary at any phase with its phase and sigma (`free_secondary_ppm`, `free_secondary_phase`, `free_secondary_sigma`), and the geometric-albedo limit A_g,max = limit x (a/R* / k) squared when the fit is ok (an occultation depth of A_g (Rp/a) squared).

### 3.6 Where it goes

- Dossier JSON (`DOSSIER_VERSION` "3"): `ephemeris` gains `period_err_days`, `t0_bkjd_err`, `t0_bjd_err`, `t_ref_bkjd`, `t_ref_bjd`, `t_ref_err_days`, `epoch_ref`, `duration_h_err` (`{"minus", "plus"}`), `depth_ppm_err`, `depth_source` ("per-event weighted mean of the box depth"), `ephemeris_method`, `period_source`, `duration_source` (ruling of 22 October 2026, Task 2: a value and its error belong to one quantity, so when the timing fit is ok `period_days`, `t0_bkjd` and `t0_bjd` are the linear fit's values, `duration_h` is the fitted T14 when the fit was used, else the bootstrap box width, else the search box, and `depth_ppm` is rung 2's depth; the search values stay in `signal`); new blocks `timing` (the per-transit table: epoch, t_bkjd, t_bjd, err_days, chi2_red, used, reason; the fit numbers; `ok` and `reason`), `shape` (k, b, a_over_r, t14_h, model_depth_ppm, each median, minus, plus; `n_boot`, `n_converged`, `seed`, `ok`, `reason`), `parity` (odd, even: depth_ppm, err_ppm, n; `difference_sigma`), `secondary` (the numbers of 3.5). `planet.rp_rearth` folds the k bootstrap error in quadrature with the stellar term.
- The page: the hero tiles print the period and the epoch with their errors in Expert; section 01's key-value list gains the errors, T_ref and the timing rms; section 02 gains the odd and even depths with errors and the secondary limit; section 03's expert view gains the shape block.
- The pack: `data/ephemeris.csv` rows carry minus and plus for every parameter that has them; `data/transit_times.csv`; `exofop/params_planet_<date>_001.txt` and `exofop/params_stellar_<date>_001.txt` in ExoFOP's pipe-delimited templates (the planet file's `target` is `TIC<tic>.01` when a TIC was given, else `TIC<TIC-TO-FILL>.01`, with `flag` `newctoi`, `disp` `PC`, the paper URL and the notes as placeholders; the README says the file is used only once a community candidate is allowed); the ExoFOP note, the archive email and the README quote P, T0 and the duration with errors; README step 5 gives the next three transits after the pack date with their 1-sigma windows; the Research Note's signal section quotes P, T_ref (BJD_TDB) and the duration with errors and the timing rms, and points to the README for how the errors were made (the methods sentence of ruling h moved there in Task 8 fix round 3); the note does not repeat the abstract's "It remains a candidate until independent follow-up confirms it." in its body, and it gives a prior claim's date and DOI once, in the body beside the citation, the abstract naming only who reported the signal first.
- The gate: `--zenodo-live` is refused when the stored dossier has no ephemeris error (`timing.ok` false) or no shape errors, with the reason.

## 4. The cross-check (`skysift crosscheck`, `tools/triceratops_check.py`)

### 4.1 Environment

TRICERATOPS 1.1.0 pulls numpy 1.26 and scipy 1.13 (dry run of 22 October), below the engine's numpy 2 requirement, so it cannot go into the engine's venv without changing the numerics the search version stands on. It goes into its own environment under the project, `.venv-triceratops` (git-ignored as `.venv-*/`), made with uv at Python 3.12, and the engine calls it as a subprocess. vespa was the alternative and is unmaintained (Python 2 era, astropy 1); TRICERATOPS is the tool TESS and K2 validations use and has a Kepler mode, so it is the choice.

### 4.2 Inputs and the adapter

`skysift crosscheck --target <id> --export <dir>` writes, from the stored dossier and the loader's whitened flux: `inputs.json` (target id, Gaia id, KIC, RA, Dec, G, the period, T0, duration and depth with errors, the stellar parameters, the Gaia neighbour list with separations and G, the neighbour KIC whose pixel files hold the star, the quarters with pixel files) and `lightcurve.csv` (time BKJD, whitened relative flux, error, quarter, within plus and minus three durations of each transit, the window the fit uses), and copies the stored pixel files of the neighbour KIC for the quarters the rung used (the files are in the blob; TRICERATOPS needs one image and a WCS per quarter).

`tools/triceratops_check.py --inputs <dir> --out result.json`, run with the other interpreter, builds a `triceratops.target` with `mission="Kepler"`, `ra` and `dec` given (which skips the KIC lookup) and `sectors=[]`, then sets the quarter images itself from the copied pixel files (the time-mean flux image, the reference pixel and the WCS-placed pixel coordinates of every star in its TIC list, exactly as the package does for a Kepler target it can download, so that the download step, which would fail for a star Kepler never targeted, is not needed). The aperture passed to `calc_depths` is the 3 x 3 box around the target in each quarter, the footprint of the KBonus PSF photometry; `dilution_corrected=True` because the PSF photometry already shares the light between stars; `exptime` is the Kepler long cadence, 0.0204 days; `P_orb` is the dossier's period; the light curve is the exported window. TRICERATOPS's TIC list is cross-matched with the dossier's Gaia neighbours within 1 arcsec; a dossier neighbour missing from the TIC list is reported in the result and added as a star with `Tmag` set from G (G minus 0.5 for a red star; stated as an estimate). The run is repeated `N_RUNS` = 10 times with different seeds and the result stores every run's FPP and NFPP plus their median, since the KOI-1623 paper found run-to-run variation (its median 1e-6 against a mean 0.0058).

`result.json`: the package version, the run date, the median and the list of FPP and NFPP, the per-scenario probability table of the median run, the stars used (id, Tmag, separation, flux ratio, required depth), the inputs' hash, the caveats (TESS-band magnitudes (Tmag) for the flux shares while the limb darkening comes from the package's own Kepler table when the mission is Kepler (corrected 22 October 2026 at the Task 4 review: the first draft said TESS-band limb darkening, which is wrong for a run made with mission "Kepler") used for a Kepler light curve; the Gaussian aperture model; the package's own priors). `skysift crosscheck --target <id> --import result.json` stores it in a new `dossiers.cross_check` JSON column (migration `004_cross_check`) with the dossier fingerprint it was made against; the page's section 03 shows a "Cross-check" block; the pack's texts quote it ("An independent estimate with TRICERATOPS (Giacalone et al. 2021) gives a false-positive probability of X and a nearby false-positive probability of Y"); `docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-22-fpp-cross-check.md` records the two stars' numbers against SkySift's with the reasons for the differences.

### 4.3 The gate

A disagreement above a factor of three does not block the live gate. Reasons: the two estimates answer differently scoped questions (TRICERATOPS counts transiting planets and binaries on nearby and bound stars that SkySift's three scenarios leave out, uses TESS magnitudes for the flux shares (Kepler limb darkening) and its own priors, and varies between runs by orders of magnitude at low FPP), so a ratio between two small numbers is noise, not a verdict. What does block: no cross-check stored, a cross-check made against another fingerprint (period, depth or neighbours changed), or a cross-check that reads as a likely false positive on the tool's own scale (median FPP at or above 0.5, or median NFPP at or above 0.1, Giacalone et al. 2021's thresholds). The pack always prints both numbers side by side with one sentence on the difference when the ratio exceeds three.

**Rulings of 22 October 2026 from the parent session's TRICERATOPS audit (section 4 revised; reasons in each).** (n) Flux sharing: the package splits the light by a Gaussian PSF over the 3 x 3 Kepler box as for aperture photometry, which does not describe KBonus PSF photometry (38 percent of the box light came from neighbours in the smoke run; the KIC 5689121 NFPP ran 0.13 to 1.0 on that artefact). After calc_depths the driver overwrites every fluxratio and tdepth: with F the Kepler-band flux of each star (Gaia G as the proxy, stated) and L_i the fraction of neighbour i's flux that the PSF fit leaks into the target's light curve (controller's reading of 22 October: SkySift's neighbour-leak test measures the dip as it appears in the neighbour's own aperture, the opposite direction, so no measured L_i exists; L_i is therefore bounded by the KBonus contamination budget, the sum of L_i F_i over F_t at most (1 - CROWDSAP) / CROWDSAP of the target's KBonus file, CROWDSAP being the target's own fraction of the aperture light so that the target's flux ratio is exactly CROWDSAP (amended 22 October after the Task 9 review: the first wording used 1 - CROWDSAP, a ratio to the aperture light, not to the target's), which the PSF fit removes to first order so it is an upper bound, shared among the neighbours in proportion to the package's own Gaussian-box fractions; the neighbour-aperture dip stays as a reported consistency check; the source and the numbers recorded per star), fluxratio_target = F_t / (F_t + sum L_i F_i), fluxratio_i = L_i F_i / (F_t + sum L_i F_i), so tdepth_i = tdepth F_t / (L_i F_i) is the eclipse depth star i would need. No star is removed on the strength of SkySift's own centroid test (that would make the cross-check circular). The spec's 'footprint of the KBonus PSF photometry' wording is withdrawn. (o) Two numbers: the full FPP and the FPP without STP, (FPP - P_STP) / (1 - P_STP), the like-for-like comparison with SkySift, whose model has no planet-on-companion scenario; no imaging exists, so an unresolved companion inside 2.2 arcsec is the open false-positive channel, stated in the dossier caveat. (p) The flux error is the robust out-of-transit scatter (commit 57a0465); every quoted run postdates it; points beyond 5 MAD are clipped from the export window and counted. (q) Neighbour stellar parameters missing from the TIC (TIC 120686466 has none) come from Gaia DR3 (gspphot Teff and radius, FLAME mass where present, else Teff from BP-RP with the Mamajek table and the mass-radius relation of the stars module), set with update_star and recorded per star with their source. (r) Light is shared in the Kepler band (Gaia G as the proxy; the KIC Kp of the target as a check); the caveat states that TESS magnitudes understate G-star neighbours' share in the Kepler band, and that the package's bound-companion and background dilution remain TESS-band. (s) Convergence: parallel=True (one seed checked against the serial loop), at least 20 seeded runs per signal, every run's FPP, NFPP, FPP without STP, P_STP, P_PTP and best scenario stored; degenerate runs excluded from the median and counted; the dossier and the pack print the median with the 16th to 84th percentile range, never the median alone. (t) Classes and the gate: Giacalone et al. 2021's lines are labels only, validated (FPP < 0.015 and NFPP < 0.001), likely planet (FPP < 0.5 and NFPP < 0.001), likely nearby false positive (NFPP > 0.1), else candidate; the caveat states that the paper found no validation region at 30-minute cadence (section 4.2), which Kepler long cadence is, so no candidate is called validated by this tool; the 'likely false positive at FPP >= 0.5' class is withdrawn; the live gate keeps only the missing-or-stale rule; a full FPP at or above 0.5 is explained in the note by naming the dominant scenario and its probability, and the README flags it for the owner. (u) Kepler limb darkening, Kepler mode with empty sectors and the Kepler pixel files are correct and kept; the methods name TRICERATOPS 1.1.0 with its Gaia DR3 field-star population and KBonus (Martinez-Palomera et al. 2023) as the light-curve source. (v) Extension: KIC 6699887 is cross-checked at both stage 2 periods (signals 3479 and 3480); the 47.69 d result is recorded in the note with its file beside it because the dossier is one per target (checklist row 27).

## 5. The centroid systematic floor (pixel rung)

### 5.1 What is measured, and why on this data

Kepler's Data Validation added a constant 0.2/3 arcsec term in quadrature to each offset component so that no offset under 0.2 arcsec could reach 3 sigma (Bryson et al. 2013, VI.3; Twicken et al. 2018, III.4.2). That constant was set for the PRF-fit estimator on Kepler targets; SkySift's estimator is a 3 x 3 flux-weighted centroid on a season frame stacked from several quarters at integer shifts, so its floor has to be measured on its own data.

The floor is measured on the live pixel files (eight stars, 129 quarter files, every star with a rung 5 row), not on the calibration set, which has no pixels. For each season frame the tool measures, per quarter, the 3 x 3 flux-weighted centroid of that quarter's out-of-transit direct image around the target's rounded frame position (data with no transit in it, high signal to noise), and the scatter of those centroids about the season's mean. That scatter is the quarter-to-quarter systematic the Monte Carlo error cannot see: pointing differences between quarters of one season, PRF changes, and the integer co-registration. The same is measured on the neighbour (the Kepler target, bright, always on the frame) as a check. Seasons with a cut box (the partial-box branch's `box_pixels` below 9) are left out of the pooled number and reported apart. The pooled root-mean-square per axis over all complete-box seasons with at least two usable quarters is the candidate floor. A second measurement validates it: on KIC 6146676 (three voting seasons, 17 sigma) the per-quarter difference-image centroids of the real transit are compared with their Monte Carlo errors; the excess scatter must not exceed the floor.

The registration residual is reported separately: per season, the event-weighted mean of each quarter's fractional offset from the first quarter's position (`residual_offsets_pix`, which the season builder already computes). If that mean exceeds the floor for any voting season of a live signal, the season's star positions are corrected to the event-weighted mean position (an exact correction from the quarters' own WCS positions, with precedent in Bryson et al. 2013 combining per-quarter offsets rather than images) and the correction is stored per season as `registration_shift_pix`; otherwise it is left as it is and recorded in the note. This is decided by the measurement, and the ruling goes in the ledger.

### 5.2 The rule

`CENTROID_FLOOR_PIX` in `lc/pixels.py`, with the measurement date and the tool that made it; `centroid_sigma_pix` becomes the quadrature sum of the Monte Carlo error, the edge allowance (from the partial-box branch) and the floor; `centroid_sigma_axis_pix` the same with the floor along the axis; the raw Monte Carlo error is kept as `centroid_sigma_mc_pix` and the floor as `floor_pix` in every season's metrics. `separation_sigma` is the bisector distance over the inflated axis error. The rung's `rule_version` becomes 3. The FPP reads the inflated error through `centroid_estimate`, so the confusion radius widens with it.

Recalibration path: `python -m tools.centroid_floor --db <copy> --root <repo> --out <json>` prints the pooled numbers and the per-season table; the constant is changed by hand with the new date, and the recheck reopens every rung 5 row whose `rule_version` is below the current one (passes and fails included: the floor changes every season's error, so a verdict that was marginal must be measured again; a pass at 17 sigma comes back as a pass with new numbers). The pixel files come from the blob cache.

### 5.3 The live run

Back up `.superpowers/sample-run/engine.db` to `engine.db.bak-<date>.db` (with its WAL checkpointed first), run `skysift recheck`, and report every rung 5 row whose status or reason changed, with the before and after numbers per season. This runs after the partial-box branch is merged into main and brought into this branch with the app's sync tool, since both change `lc/pixels.py`.


**Rulings of 22 October 2026 at the Task 6 review.** (a) The registration correction is applied to every season, not only to those whose residual exceeds the floor: the seasons that exceed it would be corrected either way, the others move by less than 0.017 px, and one rule for every season is simpler to state and to test; the floor is measured after the correction, as a scatter about the season mean, so the two never double-count. (b) The reopen by rule version covers every stored rung 5 row that holds season metrics, whatever its status; a row that never localised (no neighbour, an outage, a crash) has no error for the floor to change and is left to the next ordinary recheck. (c) The adopted floor is the larger of the target and the neighbour measurement (Bryson et al. 2013 take the larger of two error estimates): 0.0167 px per axis, 0.066 arcsec at 3.98 arcsec per pixel, the size of the DV 0.0667 arcsec term. (d) A reopened signal whose verdict is settled by the pixel rung keeps that verdict when the reopen's pixel step fails on an outage or an exception; the recheck row says skipped and the next recheck retries.

**Rulings of 22 October 2026 at the final whole-branch review.** (e) The pixel offset is quoted the way Bryson et al. 2013 (section VI) define it: the difference-image centroid against the star's own out-of-transit (direct-image) centroid, as the vector mean over the voting seasons with its error, against the 3 sigma rule and the 0.2 arcsec level; the distance from the Gaia position projected through the stamp's WCS is kept as a second number with one clause that it carries the WCS zero point and the registration (the two-source fit's common shift), so a reader does not take a catalogue-referenced 0.76 arcsec for a 5 sigma offset. (f) The cross-check export refuses a dossier below the current DOSSIER_VERSION, and the flux error handed to TRICERATOPS is the robust out-of-transit scatter of the whitened flux (1.4826 x MAD), recorded in the result's settings and named in a caveat; the result's caveats also say the Gaia neighbours are matched to the TIC by separation only (no stored RA/Dec). (g) The live Zenodo gate also refuses a dossier whose stored commit is dirty or unknown. (h) The methods are named in one sentence (linear fit of per-transit times with a central epoch, Deeg 2015; per-transit timing, Holczer et al. 2016; bootstrap over transits, Schmitt et al. 2014), printed since Task 8 fix round 3 in the README's ephemeris section with the three works' full references, while the Research Note keeps a pointer of at most 26 words without citations ("The ephemeris and transit-parameter errors come from per-transit fits, a weighted linear fit and a bootstrap over transits (methods in the Zenodo pack's README).", the first pointer naming the README as the Zenodo pack's; fix round 4) so that a note with a prior claim fits the word limit; the note prints the bootstrap median beside the point value when the shape_offset caveat fires. (i) The figure caption's bin width is the stored fold bin, not derived from the duration. (j) One precision rule for every text and file: the error to two significant figures, the value to the error's last decimal. (k) An albedo limit above 1, or one from a degenerate fit, prints as no constraint. (l) The ExoFOP planet target for a new community candidate is TIC<tic>.01 (the candidate suffix), to be confirmed against the live template by the owner. (m) Section 6's log g: mass and radius errors in quadrature, correlation ignored, the provenance file says so.

## 10. The TESS non-detection statement (added 22 October 2026 after the parent session's call)

Research: `docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-22-tess-non-detection-research.md` (every number there carries a link). The statement answers one question per stage 2 candidate: should TESS have seen this transit, and if so does its silence say anything. The rulings below are the controller's, each with its precedent.

### 10.1 What is stated
For every stage 2 signal the dossier carries a `tess` block: the TIC number and Tmag, the sectors that observed the star and their cadences, the number of Kepler-ephemeris transits that fall inside the observing windows (and the expected number from phase coverage), the Kepler depth diluted into a TESS aperture, the 1-hour noise at the diluted magnitude under a nominal and a conservative model, the per-transit and folded significance under both, the propagated timing error at the TESS epoch, and one of four verdicts: `should have detected`, `marginal`, `not expected`, `not observed in transit`. The note gets one sentence per candidate, the README one row, and the ExoFOP note one line; the archive email and the Zenodo text do not change.

### 10.2 Inputs and where they live
TESS inputs are fetched once by `tools/tess_inputs.py --target <id>` and stored in `targets.meta["tess"]` with the query date, exactly as the KBonus header values are: the dossier reads them and stays offline and reproducible. The tool resolves the star by KIC number in the TIC through MAST's invoke API (`Mast.Catalogs.Filtered.Tic`, filter `KIC`; the `/api/v0.1/Catalogs/filtered/Tic` path returns 404) and cone-searches `Mast.Catalogs.Tic.Cone` to 0.03 degrees (105 arcsec, five TESS pixels) for neighbours with `ID`, `ra`, `dec`, `Tmag`, `GAIAmag`, `gaiabp`, `gaiarp`, `dstArcSec`. A null Tmag is filled from Gaia by Stassun et al. 2019 equation 1 (T = G - 0.00522555 x^3 + 0.0891337 x^2 - 0.633923 x + 0.0324473, x = BP - RP) with the source recorded. The TIC `contratio` is null for nearly every faint Kepler-field star (2 of 71 near KIC 6146676) and is not used. Requests go through `net.fetch` (GET, logged); the invoke endpoint is called with the JSON in the `request` query parameter, and only if MAST refuses GET does `net.fetch` gain a `method` argument.

### 10.3 Sectors and windows (the record the parent asked for)
The sectors that have observed the Kepler field as of 22 October 2026 are 14, 15, 26 (Cycle 2, 30-minute FFIs), 40, 41, 53, 54, 55 (Cycle 4, 10-minute), 56 (Cycle 5, 200-second) and 74, 75, 80, 81, 82 (Cycle 6, 200-second). Cycles 1, 3, 7 and 8 did not point at the field; Cycle 5 reached its edge only in Sector 56 (amended in Task 8 fix round 1: TESScut lists Sector 56 for KIC 9243078 and KIC 10352243, though the research's four test positions miss it; its four segments, orbits 119 and 120, are BTJD 2825.251-2831.724, 2831.934-2838.513, 2838.724-2845.876 and 2846.085-2853.138 from the same FFI file, converted as the research note converts the others); Sector 109 is in progress; Sectors 119, 120, 125, 126, 127 and 132 are provisional future coverage and are never counted. Which of the 14 a given star fell on depends on its position (camera edges), so the tool asks the TESScut sector endpoint (`GET https://mast.stsci.edu/tesscut/api/v0.1/sector?ra=&dec=`) and stores the answer; the module ships the 14 sectors' observing segments in BTJD, taken from MIT's FFI observation-times file (the Sector 26 placeholder end time repaired from the orbit-times file), as a constant table with its source. BTJD = BKJD - 2167. A sector the endpoint returns that is not in the table is recorded and not counted, with a caveat saying TESS observed it but its windows are not in the table, and every text (note sentence, README row, ExoFOP line) names it "and Sector N, not assessed".

### 10.4 Transit count and the ephemeris at the TESS epoch
Transits are counted as epochs T0 + nP (the dossier's fitted ephemeris, section 3) whose mid-time falls inside a segment with at least half of T14 covered; the count is per sector. The propagated timing error at each epoch is sqrt(sigma_T0^2 + (n sigma_P)^2); when it exceeds T14 / 2 the count is marked uncertain and the expected number of transits from phase coverage (segment length summed, divided by P) is stated beside it, following Battley et al. 2021's "outside" class. Zero transits in every segment gives the verdict `not observed in transit`, which is a statement about coverage, not a non-detection.

### 10.5 Dilution
Pixel scale 21 arcsec; the PSF a 2D Gaussian of sigma 0.75 pixel (Giacalone et al. 2021 section 2.1); the aperture the five pixels of the plus shape, the target's pixel and its four edge-sharing neighbours (wording corrected 22 October: a 1.5-pixel radius on pixel centres would give nine) (between the six pixels SPOC assigns at Tmag 13, Guerrero et al. 2021, and the four the research takes as the faint floor). Each neighbour's aperture fraction is the Gaussian integral over those pixels at its offset; c = sum over neighbours of 10^(-0.4 (T_i - T_target)) f_i / f_target; depth_TESS = depth_Kepler / (1 + c); the noise is evaluated at T_total = T_target - 2.5 log10(1 + c). The hard 42 arcsec cut with unit weight (Ricker et al. 2015's two-pixel rule) is reported beside it as the two-pixel-radius sum, for comparison; it is no bound (amended in fix round 1), since a bright star just outside 42 arcsec counts in the Gaussian sum and not in this one. A neighbour without stored offsets is left out with a caveat, never placed on the target. The dossier's `depth_ppm` is the fitted, dilution-corrected Kepler depth, so no second correction is applied for the target's own KBonus crowding.

### 10.6 Noise and significance
Nominal 1-hour noise from the TIC polynomial (Stassun et al. 2018, arXiv v1 section 3.3; the `ticgen` source): ln sigma_ppm = 3.29685004771 + 0.850021465753 T - 0.285041632435 T^2 + 0.0395908321369 T^3 - 0.0022308015894 T^4 + 4.73508403525e-5 T^5, scaled to T14 as 1 / sqrt(T14 / 1 h). Conservative noise: the SPOC robust-RMS CDPP medians over the 13 Kepler-field sectors (Twicken et al. 2025; the research note's section 2.3 table, Tmag 12 to 16, interpolated linearly in Tmag; beyond the table, amended in fix round 1, the TIC polynomial times the table's excess over it at the nearer end, 24151 / 13957 = 1.730 above T = 16 and 790 / 595 = 1.328 below T = 12, and never below the nominal noise, so the conservative model stays the pessimistic one at every magnitude and duration), scaled to T14 with the empirical ratios of section 2.4 (0.752 at 2 h, 0.647 at 3 h, 0.514 at 6 h, interpolated in log T, 1 at 1 h). Per-transit significance S1 = depth_TESS / sigma(T14); folded S = sqrt(N) S1 with N the counted transits (Twicken et al. 2025 equation 3 in the single-band case). Both models are stored and printed.

### 10.7 Verdict (controller's ruling; the thresholds are cited, the wording is ours)
`should have detected` when the conservative folded S >= 7.1 (the Kepler threshold on which Christ, Montet and Fabrycky 2019 centre their TESS recovery logistic; Sullivan et al. 2015 use 7.3); `not expected` when the nominal folded S < 3 (Christ et al.'s characterisation floor); `marginal` between; `not observed in transit` when N = 0; `not assessed` (fix round 2) when TESScut lists sectors for the star but none of them is in the table, so no coverage was counted: every text says so and no method or TESS citation is printed for it. The verdict carries a caveat that no TESS light curve was examined: the statement is a prediction of detectability, not a search. A TESScut fold is not done: it returns raw FFI pixels needing our own photometry (TESS-SPOC FFI products stop at Tmag 13.5), which is not cheap, so the parent's "only if cheap" condition fails and the route is recorded as a follow-up for candidates with a `should have detected` verdict.
