## 2. Discrepancies ranked by importance

1. **The transit-shape fit is not what the note says, and its b and a/R* are not measurements (D4, D6, D8).** `fit_transit` averages the uniform-disc model over the fold-bin width (duration / 8: 22.5 min for A, 15 min for B), not over the 29.4-minute Kepler exposure the note names. Without the exposure smearing the model needs a longer ingress, which the fit gets from a high b and a small a/R*. My unbinned refit with the model integrated over 29.4 min (15 sub-samples) moves A from b 0.86, a/R* 48 to b 0.22, a/R* 93 with chi2 changing by 0.2, and fixing a/R* at the stellar 77.9 costs delta chi2 0.1 (b 0.58). For B, a/R* fixed at the stellar 38.1 costs delta chi2 1.9. So A's "b = 0.893 +0.030/-0.063, a/R* = 43.6 +9.7/-6.1" is the spread of the bootstrap inside one basin of a flat valley, not a constraint; the implied stellar density, 0.97 g/cm3, is a factor 5.7 below the Mann value 5.51, and the note's a/R* (43.6, 25.7) and the README table's a/R* (77.9, 38.1) disagree under the same name. A referee would read the fitted density as evidence that the transit is on a larger star (a blend), which is the opposite of what the pixels say. The degeneracy flag that silences B's b and a/R* is also driven by this: A escapes it only because the unsmeared fit inflates the ingress to 46 min. The radius ratio is robust (0.0474 and 0.0462 in the refit). Fix: integrate the model over the exposure, fit with a stellar-density prior (or quote b and a/R* as unconstrained), and say which a/R* each table holds.

2. **The headline false-positive probability (F4, F9).** Both abstracts carry only "a scenario estimate puts the false-positive probability at under 1 percent" (0.0035, 0.0016). The SkySift model has no bound-companion scenario and its background term is built only from the catalogued Gaia neighbours, which the centroid excludes, so the BEB weight is of order 1e-71; Morton 2012, cited as the model's basis, draws unresolved field stars inside the exclusion radius. TRICERATOPS on the same inputs gives 0.076 and 0.13, dominated by STP (0.056 and 0.097 in the median runs). The body discloses this, but the abstract does not, and "the two models answer different questions" hides that, after STP is removed, the rest of the gap is TRICERATOPS's NEB on TIC 169822655 (0.020) and NTP on TIC 120686466 (0.030), stars the SkySift centroid excludes and TRICERATOPS is not told about. The honest summary is that both tools agree the candidate is not a nearby or background eclipsing binary once the pixels are counted, and that the unresolved bound companion (5 to 10 percent) is the open channel.

3. **"An independent recovery ... in a blind sweep" (S11, B).** The 1,590-star sweep list was built by `select_cool_dwarfs.py`, whose colour cut is justified in its own docstring and in the selection note by "KIC 5689121 is an M dwarf at BP - RP 2.19"; the sweep started on 19 October 2026, after the 13 October prior claim, and works on the same public KBonus light curve. The search itself did not use the prior ephemeris, so the detection is a genuine re-detection, but the target choice was not blind. "Independent" should be dropped from the title or qualified.

4. **"1,998 null searches of this source" (F2).** In the code "source" is the data product. The nulls are the inverted and scrambled light curves of 999 other stars from the calibration pilot, a population that overlaps the sweep list in 10 stars and contains neither target, searched with the calibration detector (no masked pass, no alias resolution) rather than the sweep detector that produced the candidates' SDE. Every exceedance for A (11 of 11) and 37 of 38 for B come from the inverted ("systematics as dips") null. The counts and the add-one rule reproduce exactly; the wording does not describe them.

5. **"The independent TRICERATOPS cross-check" and "run on the KBonus light curve" (F10, F6).** The package and its priors are independent; the inputs are not. TRICERATOPS receives SkySift's whitened flux in transit windows with 5-MAD clipping against a box model (10 in-transit points removed for B), SkySift's linear ephemeris and per-event box depth, SkySift's neighbour list and parameters, and flux shares that overwrite the package's own. Any error in the whitening, the depth or the ephemeris propagates into both FPPs.

6. **"The centroid agrees with the hand-built localisation within 0.03 px" (P8).** The comparison is between scalar distances from the star, season by season, so two centroids on opposite sides of the star at equal distance would "agree". The hand-built study used the same pixel files and the search ephemeris. The agreement shows the engine reproduces the script, not an independent localisation.

## 3. Methodological weaknesses where the text is accurate

- **W1. Depths on a superseded ephemeris.** The depth, per-event depths, parity, bootstrap and rung 3 all fold on the search period and epoch (disclosed in the README). For B the search ephemeris drifts from the linear fit by up to about 17 min at the last epochs (period difference 9.5e-5 d per epoch over about 103 epochs, t0 offset 3.4 min); a plain box fold gives 2,095 ± 80 ppm at the search ephemeris and 2,208 ± 81 ppm at the linear fit, about 5 percent and 1.4 sigma. For A the drift is under about 10 min and the fold changes by 22 ppm.
- **W2. Point values outside the middle of their own bootstrap.** The quoted centre is the full-data fit and the errors are p50 - p16 and p84 - p50 of the resamples. For B the point k (0.0461) sits at about the 16th percentile of its resamples (median 0.0477), T14 2.43 h against a resample median 2.79 h, model depth 2,124 against 2,256 ppm, so the quoted interval (k 0.0446 to 0.0512) is not the resample interval (0.0462 to 0.0528). The planet radius 2.54 +0.29/-0.11 inherits this.
- **W3. No limb darkening.** At the high impact parameters the fit prefers, a uniform disc mis-states k by a few percent; with b unconstrained (item 1) the sign of the bias is unknown, and the planet radius error does not include it.
- **W4. Aperture dependence of the depth.** Rung 3 measures the SAP depth at 0.70 (A, -3.9 sigma) and 0.63 (B, -4.7 sigma) of the PSF depth, below the CROWDSAP values 0.86 and 0.75. The rung only fails outside a factor 2, and the dossier the README points readers to says "the two apertures agree". The notes report the season test only.
- **W5. B's leak test is a 3.1 sigma dip.** 130 ± 42 ppm in the optimal aperture of KIC 5689116 is reported without the depth expected from the target's own light leaking into that aperture. It is probably the target's PSF wing (the neighbour is 1.7 px away and 2.1 times brighter in G), but the note should say so with a number.
- **W6. A's self-referenced centroid test has little power.** The direct-image centroid the test references sits 0.17, 0.50, 1.09 and 1.30 px from the Gaia position in the four seasons, pulled by the G 12.8 neighbour 2.8 px away, so the offset (1.8 arcsec) and its scatter-driven error (1.0 arcsec) mostly measure that contamination. "Not an offset by the rule" is correct but uninformative; only the Gaia-referenced 0.25 ± 0.28 arcsec constrains the source.
- **W7. B's pixels rest mostly on cut boxes.** The target's 3 x 3 box is missing 3 of 9 pixels in seasons 0, 2 and 3; of the three voting seasons only season 1 has a complete box.
- **W8. The SkySift scenario model is thin.** One on-target binary configuration (R2/R1 = M2/M1 = 0.5), BEB only for catalogued neighbours, no bound companions, no diluted-planet scenario. A code oddity, inactive here: for a fitted b of 0.9 or more `S_v = 0.5` halves the EB weights, which makes a V-shaped (grazing) transit look less like a binary; A's b of 0.893 sits just below that switch.
- **W9. Look-elsewhere across the sweep.** The FAP is per star. The sweep searched 1,590 stars (1,622 targets done in the database); at B's per-star FAP of 0.0195 about 30 noise peaks of that strength are expected in the sweep, at A's 0.006 about 10. The calibration population is also not the sweep population (10 of 999 overlap).
- **W10. TRICERATOPS set-up choices.** Clipping in-transit points at 5 raw MAD against a box (B: 10 points) pushes the shape towards a box and against grazing binaries; the target's TIC parameters (Teff 3,694 K, R 0.494) are used instead of SkySift's (3,653 K, 0.505); the whole CROWDSAP contamination budget is given to one neighbour by Gaussian-box shares in G (0.1615 of 0.1675 to the G 12.8 star, all 0.338 to the G 15.5 star), which makes NEB and NTP on those stars as easy as possible. Each is defensible and documented, but the NFPP is then a bound, not an estimate.
- **W11. Timing errors.** The per-transit template holds the depth and shape fixed. A's reduced chi2 of 0.62 means its timing errors are overestimated and are never deflated (inflation is max(1, ...)), so its period error is conservative; B's 2.04 is inflated by 1.43 and its three outliers (up to 11.9 MAD) are dropped without a look at transit-timing variations.
- **W12. Off-phase dips.** The 3.8 sigma (A, phase 0.38) and 4.0 sigma (B, phase 0.73) dips from the free secondary search are reported without a trials correction or a check for an eccentric-binary secondary.
- **W13. Season test against the global depth.** The chi2 uses the overall depth as a fixed mean with 3 degrees of freedom; minor.
- **W14. TESS prediction sensitivity.** The KBonus depth is taken as the intrinsic depth before TESS dilution. If the PSF light curve still carried the CROWDSAP dilution, the intrinsic depths would be 2,553 and 2,718 ppm and the nominal folded S/N 1.04 (A) and 2.77 (B); B would then sit just under the "marginal" threshold of 3.
- **W15. Reproducibility at the cited commit.** The search and the calibration ran at commits before fb9b2e1; only the search-version string (5+6cfe4cf4, calibration 5+42061727) links them to the code the pack cites.
